CooccurrenceRegression.LogOddsFNCH — Type
LogOddsFNCH(m_i, m_j, N, α)

Fisher's noncentral hypergeometric distribution of the number of co-occurrences between two species with prevalences m_i and m_j across N sites, parameterised by the log odds ratio (affinity) α.

Equivalent to FisherNoncentralHypergeometric(m_i, N - m_i, m_j, exp(α)) from Distributions.jl. logpdf is evaluated by fnch_logpdf, so it is stable for extreme α and has closed-form derivative rules. Can be used directly in a Turing model:

k[i, j] ~ LogOddsFNCH(m[i], m[j], N, α)

CooccurrenceRegression.affinity_mle — Method
affinity_mle(k, m_i, m_j, N)

Maximum likelihood estimate of the affinity (log odds ratio) α of two species that occupy m_i and m_j of N sites and co-occur at k of them: the value at which the expected number of co-occurrences equals k (Mainali et al. 2022).

The estimate is Inf when k is the largest value possible given the prevalences and -Inf when it is the smallest. It is NaN when only one value of k is possible, in which case the data say nothing about α. See affinity_map for an estimate that is always finite.

CooccurrenceRegression.affinity_posterior — Method
affinity_posterior(k, m_i, m_j, N; prior_sd=4.0, level=0.95, npoints=1001)

Posterior summary of the affinity α of two species that occupy m_i and m_j of N sites and co-occur at k of them, under the prior α ~ Normal(0, prior_sd).

Returns a named tuple with the posterior median and mean, the limits lower and upper of the central credible interval of probability level, and pd, the probability of direction: the posterior probability that α has the same sign as its median; and p_positive, the posterior probability that α > 0.

The posterior is one-dimensional and is integrated numerically on a grid of npoints values, so no sampling is involved and the result is deterministic.

CooccurrenceRegression.cooccurrence_counts — Method
cooccurrence_counts(Y; occupied_only=false) -> (K, m, N)

Summarise an N × S presence/absence matrix Y (rows are sites, columns are species; any value greater than zero counts as a presence).

Returns the S × S matrix K of co-occurrence counts (K[i, j] is the number of sites at which species i and j were both present), the vector m of prevalences (the number of sites at which each species was present) and the number of sites N.

With occupied_only=true, sites at which none of the species was present are left out, so that N is the number of sites occupied by at least one of them.

CooccurrenceRegression.cooccurrence_regression — Function
cooccurrence_regression(X, Y, [n_iter=1000]; kwargs...)
cooccurrence_regression(Xs::Vector, Y, [n_iter=1000]; kwargs...)

Bayesian dyadic regression of co-occurrence affinity on one or more pairwise explanatory variables.

Y is an N × S presence/absence matrix with sites in rows and species in columns. X is an S × S matrix whose element X[i, j] relates species i to species j (for example a trait or phylogenetic distance); only the lower triangle is used. Several explanatory variables are passed as a vector of such matrices, Xs.

For each pair of species i > j, with prevalences m[i], m[j] and k[i, j] co-occurrences,

k[i, j] ~ LogOddsFNCH(m[i], m[j], N, α + λ[i] + λ[j] + Σₚ β[p] * Xs[p][i, j])
α    ~ Normal(0, alpha_sd)
β[p] ~ Normal(0, beta_sd)
λ[i] ~ Normal(0, lambda_sd)

where LogOddsFNCH is Fisher's noncentral hypergeometric distribution parameterised by the log odds ratio (see LogOddsFNCH). The species effects λ account for the non-independence of pairs that share a species. Species present at none or at all of the sites carry no information and their pairs are left out of the likelihood.

Sampling uses NUTS and returns an MCMCChains.Chains object with parameters α, β[p] (in the order the explanatory variables were given) and λ[i] (in the order of the columns of Y).

Keyword arguments

  • alpha_sd=4.0, beta_sd=1.0, lambda_sd=1.0: prior standard deviations.

  • n_adapts=100: number of adaptation steps.

  • delta=0.65: target acceptance rate.

  • adtype: automatic differentiation backend (an ADTypes type). By default chosen from the number of parameters, see default_adtype.

  • strata: a vector giving the type of each site (row of Y), for data collected from several kinds of site whose differences should not contribute to affinity. Prevalences and co-occurrences are then counted within each site type l, with N[l] sites:

    k[i, j, l] ~ LogOddsFNCH(m[i, l], m[j, l], N[l], α[l] + λ[i, l] + λ[j, l] + Σₚ β[p] * Xs[p][i, j])

    The site types share the coefficients β and each has its own intercept α[l] and species effects λ[i, l] (types in sorted order).

  • metric=:affinity: the measure of co-occurrence being modelled. With metric=:jaccard the linear predictor is logit(J[i, j]), where J is the Jaccard index (the probability that both species occur at a site where at least one does), and the likelihood is k[i, j] ~ Binomial(m[i] + m[j] - k[i, j], J[i, j]) (see jaccard_logpdf). Unlike affinity, J depends on how common the two species are.

  • site_weights=false: if true, sites are not treated as equally available. The likelihood is the site-weighted one, with site effects from a logistic model with species and site effects, re-estimated for each pair with that pair left out (see site_weighted_pairs), so that differences in richness between sites do not appear as affinity. Known site effects can be given instead as a vector, one per row of Y. Cannot be combined with strata.

  • drop_lambda=false: if true, the species effects are left out of the returned chain.

Example

using CooccurrenceRegression
Y = rand(Bool, 30, 12)                 # 30 sites, 12 species
t = randn(12)                          # a trait
X = abs.(t .- t')                      # pairwise trait distance
chn = cooccurrence_regression(X, Y)

CooccurrenceRegression.cooccurrence_regression — Function
cooccurrence_regression(X, Y, g::AbstractVector, [n_iter=1000]; kwargs...)
cooccurrence_regression(Xs::Vector, Y, g::AbstractVector, [n_iter=1000]; kwargs...)

Hierarchical dyadic regression within groups. g assigns each species (column of Y) to a group, such as a genus or a functional guild. Only pairs of species from the same group enter the likelihood, and each group q has its own regression coefficients, drawn from a common distribution:

k[i, j] ~ LogOddsFNCH(m[i], m[j], N, α + λ[i] + λ[j] + Σₚ b[p, q] * Xs[p][i, j])   for g[i] == g[j] == q
b[p, q] = βμ[p] + βσ[p] * β[p, q]
β[p, q] ~ Normal(0, 1)
βμ[p]   ~ Normal(0, mu_sd)
βσ[p]   ~ Gamma(sigma_params[1], sigma_params[2])     (shape, scale)
α       ~ Normal(0, alpha_sd)
λ[i]    ~ Normal(0, lambda_sd)

The returned chain holds α, βμ, βσ, the standardised deviations β[p, q] (groups in sorted order) and λ. The coefficient of group q is βμ[p] + βσ[p] * β[p, q]; βμ is the average relationship across groups. The species effects λ are ordered by group and then by column of Y.

With pooled=true the groups share a single set of coefficients β[p] ~ Normal(0, beta_sd) instead, so that the model is the non-hierarchical one restricted to pairs within groups.

By default the prevalence of a species and the number of sites N refer to all sites, so the model asks whether two species are found together more often than expected if each could occur anywhere. With group_range=true the sites considered for a group are those at which at least one of its species is present, so that co-occurrence is judged within the range of the group. Use this when groups occupy different regions and absence from places a group has never reached should not count as evidence.

Keyword arguments are as for the non-hierarchical method (including metric), with mu_sd=1.0 and sigma_params=(2.0, 0.1) in place of beta_sd.

CooccurrenceRegression.default_adtype — Method
default_adtype(n_params)

AD backend used when adtype is not given: ForwardDiff for models with at most FORWARD_MODE_MAX_PARAMS parameters, Mooncake (reverse mode) otherwise. The dyadic model has one parameter per species plus the intercept and regression coefficients, so communities of more than a few dozen species use Mooncake.

CooccurrenceRegression.fnch_logpdf — Method
fnch_logpdf(k, m_i, m_j, N, α)

Log-probability of k co-occurrences between two species with prevalences m_i and m_j across N sites, given log odds ratio (affinity) α:

log P(K = k | α) = log c(k) + α k - log Σᵤ c(u) exp(α u),
c(u) = binomial(m_i, u) * binomial(N - m_i, m_j - u).

Equivalent to logpdf(FisherNoncentralHypergeometric(m_i, N - m_i, m_j, exp(α)), k) from Distributions.jl, but evaluated in log space so that it stays finite and accurate for extreme α and for k far in the tails, and never throws during sampling.

Derivative rules with respect to α are provided for ChainRules-compatible automatic differentiation, ForwardDiff and Mooncake, using the closed form k - E[K | α].

source
CooccurrenceRegression.fnch_logpdf_and_gradient — Method
fnch_logpdf_and_gradient(k, m_i, m_j, N, α) -> (logp, dlogp_dα)

Log-probability of observing k co-occurrences between two species with prevalences m_i and m_j across N sites under Fisher's noncentral hypergeometric distribution with log odds ratio (affinity) α, together with its derivative with respect to α.

The derivative is k - E[K | α], the observed minus the expected number of co-occurrences. Both values come from a single pass over the support.

Never throws for valid data: α = ±Inf returns the limiting point-mass values, α = NaN and k outside the support return (-Inf, 0).

See also fnch_logpdf, fnch_mean.

source
CooccurrenceRegression.fnch_mean — Method
fnch_mean(m_i, m_j, N, α)

Expected number of co-occurrences E[K | α] between two species with prevalences m_i and m_j across N sites, given log odds ratio (affinity) α. At α = 0 this is the hypergeometric expectation m_i * m_j / N.

CooccurrenceRegression.jaccard_logpdf — Method
jaccard_logpdf(k, m_i, m_j, N, η)

Log-probability of k co-occurrences under the binomial model for the Jaccard index, with η = logit(J). See jaccard_logpdf_and_gradient. Has closed-form derivative rules, like fnch_logpdf, and takes the same arguments, so either can be used as the likelihood of the regression (metric = :jaccard in cooccurrence_regression).

CooccurrenceRegression.jaccard_logpdf_and_gradient — Method
jaccard_logpdf_and_gradient(k, m_i, m_j, N, η) -> (logp, dlogp_dη)

Log-probability of k co-occurrences between two species with prevalences m_i and m_j across N sites, given η = logit(J), where J is the Jaccard index: the probability that both species occur at a site where at least one of them does. With u = m_i + m_j - k sites holding at least one of the two, k ~ Binomial(u, J). The derivative is k - u * J.

Never throws for valid data: η = ±Inf returns the limiting values, η = NaN and k outside the possible range return (-Inf, 0).

CooccurrenceRegression.pairwise_affinity — Method
pairwise_affinity(Y; prior_sd=4.0, level=0.95, site_weights=false, metric=:affinity)

Posterior summaries of the affinity of every pair of species in the N × S presence/absence matrix Y (rows are sites, columns are species), each pair analysed on its own with the prior α ~ Normal(0, prior_sd).

With site_weights=true the likelihood is the site-weighted one, with site effects estimated for each pair with that pair left out (see site_weighted_pairs), so that differences in richness between sites do not appear as associations. Known site effects can be given instead as a vector, site_weights = b, one per row of Y.

With metric=:jaccard the summaries are of logit(J) instead, where J is the Jaccard index of the pair, under the binomial model of jaccard_logpdf and the prior logit(J) ~ Normal(0, prior_sd). Not available with site weights.

Returns a named tuple of symmetric S × S matrices: median, lower, upper (credible interval of probability level), pd (probability of direction) and p_positive (probability that the affinity is positive). Entries are NaN on the diagonal and for species present at none or all of the sites.

See affinity_posterior for a single pair.

CooccurrenceRegression.site_effects — Method
site_effects(Y; prior_sd=10.0, tol=1e-8, maxiter=1000)

Fit logit P(Y[n, s] = 1) = a[s] + b[n], a logistic model with a species effect a[s] and a site effect b[n], to the N × S presence/absence matrix Y (rows are sites, columns are species), with weak Normal(0, prior_sd) priors on all effects so that the estimates exist for perfectly nested matrices.

Sites at which none or all of the species occur, and species present at none or all of the sites, carry no information about the effects and are removed first (repeatedly, until none remain). Returns a named tuple with the indices of the remaining sites and species, their effects b and a (posterior modes), and D, the curvature Σₛ p(1 - p) + 1/prior_sd² of the log posterior for each site effect, which site_weighted_pairs uses to remove a pair from the fit.

CooccurrenceRegression.site_weighted_pairs — Method
site_weighted_pairs(Y; b=nothing, prior_sd=10.0) -> Vector{WeightedPair}

Data for the site-weighted co-occurrence model of every informative pair of species in the N × S presence/absence matrix Y (rows are sites, columns are species).

By default the site effects are estimated with site_effects. The richness of a site includes the two species whose co-occurrence is being assessed, which biases affinity downwards, so for each pair the site effects are re-estimated with that pair removed, by one Newton step from the fit to all species. Sites holding none or all of the species, and species present at none or all of the sites, are removed first: the first have weight zero and the second weight infinity in the limit, so they carry no information about any pair.

Alternatively, known site effects can be supplied as b, one per row of Y; all sites are then used with weights exp.(b), and no pair is left out.

CooccurrenceRegression.standardise — Method
standardise(X, g)

Standardise within groups: for each group in g (one entry per species), the off-diagonal elements of X between species of that group are centred and scaled to mean 0 and standard deviation 1. Elements between species of different groups, which are not used by the within-group models of cooccurrence_regression, are set to 0, as are the elements of a group whose pairs all share one value (any group of two species).

CooccurrenceRegression.standardise — Method
standardise(X)

Centre and scale a square matrix of pairwise values so that its off-diagonal elements have mean 0 and standard deviation 1. The diagonal, which plays no part in the regression, is set to 0. Standardising explanatory variables puts their coefficients on a common scale, which the default priors of cooccurrence_regression assume.

CooccurrenceRegression.swap_null — Function
swap_null(Y, [n_trades]; rng=Random.default_rng())

A random presence/absence matrix with the same number of species at every site (row totals) and the same prevalence for every species (column totals) as Y, but no associations beyond those the totals impose. Generated by the curveball algorithm (Strona et al. 2014): in each trade two sites exchange a random subset of the species that only one of them holds.

Use it to check an analysis: fitted to such matrices, a sound analysis should find few associations. The default n_trades is 10 * max(N, S) for an N × S matrix.

trait_distance(x)

CooccurrenceRegression.weighted_coefficients — Method
weighted_coefficients(y_i, m_j, w) -> (lo, logc)

Log coefficients of the site-weighted co-occurrence distribution of two species, conditional on the sites at which species i occurs (y_i, a Bool vector over sites) and on the prevalence m_j of species j, with site weights w:

log c(u) = log e_u(w at sites with i) + log e_{m_j - u}(w at sites without i),

for u = lo, lo + 1, …, up to min(m_i, m_j), shifted so that the largest is 0. With equal weights these are the FNCH coefficients binomial(m_i, u) * binomial(N - m_i, m_j - u) up to a constant. Use with weighted_logpdf.

weighted_logpdf_and_gradient(k, lo, logc, α) -> (logp, dlogp_dα)

Log-probability of k co-occurrences under the exponential family P(K = u) ∝ exp(logc[u - lo + 1] + α u), and its derivative k - E[K | α] with respect to α.
